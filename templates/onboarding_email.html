You may know us through one program or one donation. But KSE is much bigger than any single project.

KSE brings together a university, a foundation, a research institute, a business school, and vocational training through KSE ProfTech. Together, these institutions build the capacity Ukraine needs through education, research, strong institutions, practical skills, and direct support.

KSE takes 0.3% of everyone who enters university in Ukraine — and 19% of the STEM olympiad winners who stay in the country. This year’s new students came in with the highest entrance scores in Ukraine, and half of them chose STEM. They join over 2,000 students, the largest community in KSE’s 30 years.

KSE Foundation is one of Ukraine’s five largest charitable foundations, according to Forbes Ukraine. It strengthens Ukraine by investing in advanced education, science and talent, supporting KSE’s institutional development and expanding access to transformative learning. This academic year, donors fund scholarships for 879 KSE students. The Foundation also leads nationwide educational, humanitarian and defence initiatives, from school shelters and talent grants to evacuation support and critical equipment for Ukraine’s defenders.

KSE Institute is one of the largest think tanks in Ukraine. Its research supports decisions on Ukraine’s economy, recovery, investment, and sanctions. The Institute tracks russian oil revenues, the shadow fleet, and sanctions evasion, and works with government and international partners on Ukraine’s recovery.

KSE ProfTech prepares people for the jobs Ukraine needs now through short vocational programs designed together with employers. 83% of graduates are employed after training.

KSE Business School is led by former Prime Minister Yulia Svyrydenko and develops leaders for business, public institutions, and the defence sector. Its programs also work directly with Ukraine’s defence industry.
